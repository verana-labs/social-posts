## Content

### LinkedIn

Who is this agent? Who operates it? What is it allowed to do? And can you verify all of that without a central platform watching every interaction?

We just published The Agent Identity Landscape, September 2026: a comparative analysis of trust approaches for autonomous actors, written with Ariel Gentile.

It maps six deployed families of solutions: enterprise agent IAM (Microsoft Entra Agent ID, Okta, AWS AgentCore), agent directories (A2A Agent Cards, MCP Registry, AGNTCY, ANS), bot attestation (Web Bot Auth), on-chain registries (ERC-8004), payment agent programs (Visa TAP, Google AP2, Mastercard), and trust registry networks (Ayra, DeDi).

Each is evaluated against four criteria: who it is, who operates it, what it may do, and no central observer.

The finding: every family solves part of the problem, and each is valuable in its own domain. But none binds an agent to an accountable operator, with verifiable authorizations, across domains, without an observer. That last mile is exactly what a public, decentralized trust layer is for, and the paper closes with Verifiable Trust as a working, open-source implementation, live on the Verana testnet.

Open access, CC BY 4.0. We would love your scrutiny: corrections and challenges welcome on the repo.

🔗 Read the paper: https://verana-labs.github.io/verana-research/papers/agent-identity-landscape-2026/landscape-paper.html
