## Content

### LinkedIn (personal)

My AI writes a contract. I send it to you. Your AI reviews it and finds 12 issues. Your AI rewrites. My AI reviews the rewrite and finds 9 new issues. Rewrites. Your AI finds 7 more.

Five rounds later, the document is different, not better. And nobody has decided anything.

I call it the AI ping-pong effect, and it is quietly eating the productivity AI was supposed to create. Same story in code review, security audits, specs, procurement. The rally never ends, because a language model will always find something: that is what we trained it to do. Iteration became free, so nothing forces convergence anymore.

Here is the uncomfortable part: the loop is not a technical failure. It is an accountability failure. Each side uses AI to avoid the one thing AI cannot do: take responsibility.

The scarce skill of the AI era is HI: Human Intelligence, applied at four moments.

1. Before the loop: a human defines what done means. Acceptance criteria, risk tolerance, deal-breakers.
2. At every AI disagreement: when two models disagree, the question is judgment, not fact. Escalate to a human.
3. When rounds change style, not substance: a human calls convergence. Good enough is a human decision.
4. At the end, always: sign-off. A model cannot own an outcome or carry liability. Only a human can end the rally.

AI detects. HI decides. AI generates. HI terminates.

And one look ahead: when the players are no longer two humans with copilots but autonomous agents negotiating at machine speed, the rally becomes infinite, unless identity and accountability are built into the loop itself. That is a story for another post.

What is the longest AI ping-pong rally you have survived? I will start: five rounds, and in the end one comma actually mattered.
