## Content

### LinkedIn

The EU's reference wallet. Switzerland's swiyu. The Netherlands' NL Wallet. British Columbia's BC Wallet. MOSIP's Inji. Twelve open-source credential wallets, and every one of them now answers the same three questions before you connect, accept, or share.

On the Verana Playground, each wallet runs the same six scenarios against the same live services on the Verana testnet: three issuers, three verifiers, and only one of each deserves a green light. The wallet changes; the verdict does not. Trust lives in a public layer, not in any one app.

Below, MOSIP's open-source Inji Wallet shows what that looks like: a green Proof-of-Trust for the accredited issuer, and a hard stop for the impostor, before anything about you is shared.

All twelve are open source, and that is what made this possible: where a wallet does not yet speak Verifiable Trust natively, we integrated it ourselves and published the build, the fork, and the captures. If your stack speaks OpenID4VC, a trust decision is one call to the public resolver.

And the same public registry that answers those checks also runs the business model: ecosystems publish their credential schemas and set their fees, issuers are paid when their credentials are reused, and verifiers pay per check. Easy to integrate, and a foundation for monetizable trust networks.

🔗 Building a wallet? Your listing is a pull request away: https://github.com/verana-labs/playground
